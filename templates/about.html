Problem Statement & Motivation

Agriculture accounts for approximately 70% of global freshwater withdrawals. Conventional irrigation in developing countries is predominantly managed through rigid time schedules or manual visual inspection of the field.

This manual approach leads to two severe failure modes:

  • Over-Irrigation: Leaches vital nutrients below the root zone, promotes root asphyxiation and fungal growth, wastes pumped electricity, and rapidly depletes groundwater tables.
  • Under-Irrigation: Causes agricultural drought stress, permanently damages plant cellular structure, and stunts crop harvest yields.

Proposed Solution: An intelligent Machine Learning classification system that analyzes key agricultural and atmospheric variables (Soil Moisture, Ambient Temperature, Relative Humidity, and Precipitation) to accurately predict whether irrigation is required, recommending exact water volume and application windows.

Agro-Meteorological Principles

1. Soil Moisture Dynamic

Measures volumetric water content ($\theta$). Between Field Capacity (FC) and the Permanent Wilting Point (PWP), plants easily extract moisture. Below the Management Allowed Depletion (MAD ≈ 28-30%), irrigation is triggered.

2. Evapotranspiration (ET)

Crop water loss is driven by thermal energy and vapor pressure deficit (VPD). High temperature combined with dry air (<35% RH) increases transpiration exponentially, accelerating soil water depletion.

3. Effective Rainfall ($P_{eff}$)

Not all precipitation reaches crop roots. Rainfall > 10 mm replenishes topsoil and temporarily suppresses irrigation demand regardless of prior moisture depletion.

Machine Learning Algorithms & Selection

Decision Tree Classifier

Constructs recursive orthogonal decision boundaries based on feature Gini impurity reduction. Highly interpretable, mirrors real-world agronomist heuristics (e.g. If soil_moisture < 28% and rain == 0, then irrigate).

Random Forest Classifier (Ensemble)

Aggregates an ensemble of 100 decorrelated decision trees using bootstrap aggregating (bagging) and random feature subsets. Drastically minimizes overfitting variance and captures subtle non-linear climate interactions with high generalization accuracy.

Logistic Regression

A linear probabilistic classifier mapped through the sigmoid activation function $P(y=1|X) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\beta_0 + \mathbf{\beta}^T \mathbf{X})}}$. Serves as an essential baseline and demonstrates the necessity of non-linear tree models for complex weather thresholds.
